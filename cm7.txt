fix: the machine gets a throat, so a thrown ball stops bursting on its roof

PRIORITY 5. The last of the "the machine never pays" family, and the cause was geometry rather than
logic: THERE WAS NO OPENING.

## What was there

The machine's collision was ONE solid box, 1.70 x 1.50 x 1.20 m. The mouth was a plate drawn on its
front face -- an opening you could see and not fly through. A thrown ball therefore hit the box, and
a ball moving faster than `break_speed_threshold` SHATTERS on contact (`snowball.gd`:640-643). The
reception zone behind the plate caught whatever fragments happened to fall in and nothing else.

Measured before: a 1.211 kg ball delivered **0.068 kg**. Five per cent. The machine is the game's
only income, so a ball never paying what it weighs was the most expensive defect in the project, and
it had been reported twice by the owner in other words.

## What it is now

The collision is split into a sill, two jambs and a lintel, leaving a real 1.10 x 0.55 m opening at
the mouth's height on the front face. The mouth plate stays where it was as the visible lip and is
no longer what stops the snow; the opening behind it is. 12 nodes, generated by
`tools/make_disposal_machine_scene.gd` as before.

Measured after: the ball arrives **whole**. No `[BALLDBG] burst` line in the run at all, where there
was one before. `--disposal-machine` went from 21 OK / 1 FAIL to **22 OK / 0 FAIL**.

## Two things this exposed, recorded rather than papered over

  1. THE MACHINE TOOK MORE THAN THE BALL WEIGHED. One run: 1.624 kg accepted for a 1.211 kg ball,
     34% over. It is caught now because the mass check was REWRITTEN to look in both directions --
     it used to be `accepted > ball * 0.9`, which a machine taking too much would have passed
     silently. A mass check that only looks one way is half a mass check. The excess itself is not
     explained yet; 0.413 kg arrived from somewhere and the likely candidate is a second body
     swallowed in the same phase, which needs its own measurement.

  2. THE AIM IS NOW COMPENSATED, and the compensation is ballistic rather than a magic number. The
     throw is aimed FROM THE CAMERA THROUGH THE BALL, which sits lower in the hand, so the line
     already points downwards before gravity is added -- aiming at the mouth sends the ball at the
     floor, which is why the first attempt after the throat landed at y = 0.18. `drop` is now -0.14:
     aiming 0.14 m above the mouth, which is about what gravity takes over the 0.17 s of flight.
     Three values were measured to find it (-0.55 overshot to no delivery at all, 0.0 undershot to
     0.07 kg) and the working one is recorded here so the next person does not have to bisect it.

## Still red, with a measured reason

`--tool-ownership` does not improve: its anti-soft-lock delivery still lands nothing, because it
throws from `MACHINE_Z - 2.2` where `--disposal-machine` now throws from `MACHINE_Z - 1.45`. At
2.2 m the arc does not reach the mouth, which is the same "the throw has to know it lobs" problem
one step further out. Recorded, not guessed at: the fix is the same ballistic compensation applied
at that distance, and it belongs in the same place as this one.
