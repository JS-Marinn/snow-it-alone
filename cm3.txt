fix: the machine finally catches a thrown ball, and stops asking for one exact number

Two defects, one in the machine and one in the test that was supposed to be watching it. Both were
found by a crash audit, and between them they account for the two batteries this project has had in
red the longest -- the ones filed under "the anti-soft-lock chain".

## The reception zone was inside the machine's own collision box

`_build_reception` centred the Area3D on the machine's ORIGIN, at `(0, 1.15, 0)`. The machine's own
collision box is 1.7 x 1.5 x 1.2 m centred at y = 0.75, so it spans z = -0.6 to +0.6 -- and the
zone has a 1.1 m radius. The zone was buried inside the solid body it belongs to. A thrown ball hit
that box and stopped there, and the machine never paid for it, while a chunk tipped in by hand
still did. That difference is why the failure looked like a payout bug.

The function's OWN docstring said "an Area3D in front of the machine, not the machine's own
collision box" from the day it was written. The code did not do that. It does now, at `MOUTH_Z`,
with `MOUTH_Z` and `MOUTH_Y` as constants matched to the scene so the zone and the visual cannot
drift apart.

## A fast body can cross the zone between two physics steps

`body_entered` fires on the CROSSING. The machine does not move and a thrown ball arrives in a
handful of frames, so the reliable test is the one that asks every frame rather than the one that
waits to be told. `_physics_process` now calls `get_overlapping_bodies()` and feeds each body
through the same handler, with the existing `_handled` list keeping it from paying twice for the
body the signal already caught.

Measured: before this, the thrown ball was never seen at all and produced no payment. After it,
`0.068 kg sent ... paid 1 coins`. The original brief for this machine listed "check the body's
position each frame against the mouth" as the fallback if the reception zone struggled with fast
bodies. It struggled. This is that fallback.

## The test was demanding one exact number

`anti-soft-lock: with hands only, carrying a ball to the machine raises the balance` asserted
`earned == ceil(ball_mass * PAYOUT)` EXACTLY. A thrown ball shatters into chunks, so the fragment
that landed paid 1 coin and the check called that a failure -- while the balance had, in fact,
gone up, which is the whole property being tested. The brief asks for the balance to RISE with
hands alone; bolting an exact number onto it makes the test brittle against a ball breaking, and a
ball breaking is a legitimate thing for a thrown ball to do.

It is now three checks that are each true for a reason: the balance rose; the coins match the
declared payout for what the machine's own ledger says it TOOK; and the mass that arrived is the
mass that was thrown, within the project's tolerance.

21 OK / 1 FAIL, from 19 OK / 2 FAIL.

## The failing check is a real bug, and it is now named

  the thrown ball reaches the machine as mass and none of it is lost

A 1.211 kg ball delivers 0.068 kg. The missing 1.143 kg is not destroyed and not mis-accounted: the
ball SHATTERS against the machine's collision box and the pieces land outside the mouth. A 1.1 m
reception sphere behind a 1.5 m tall solid body catches the fragments that happen to fall in and
nothing else.

So a ball thrown at this machine never pays what it weighs, and the loss scales with the ball. That
is a design question rather than a typo, and it has at least three readings: soften the collision
so the ball lands intact; put the mouth in front of the box instead of level with it; or accept the
shatter and have the machine's zone reach far enough to collect the pieces. Recorded here instead
of guessed at.
